# Section-Specific Scientific Writing

Use this document only while drafting or revising one ARIS paper section. Load
the guidance for the active surface, not as a manuscript-wide review checklist.
The current Human instruction, ARIS paper contracts, stable interfaces,
reference ledger, publication contract, and durable decisions always take
precedence.

## Core boundary

- Do not invent or strengthen contributions, facts, results, citations, or
  external-platform outcomes.
- Keep the paper's explicit observational and non-causal qualifications.
- Do not turn a writing edit into a change to claim strength, experiment
  interpretation, limitations, terminology, or publication policy.
- Retrieve the relevant contract, ledger, section, and interface before drafting.
- Keep unresolved evidence visible rather than filling it with plausible prose.

## Before a section

Identify the section's reader task, the supported claims and evidence available,
the terms and macros it must preserve, and what it must establish for the next
section. Prefer a small sequence of rhetorical moves over a sentence-by-sentence
template.

## Title and abstract

Name the actual topic and contribution within the strongest supported scope.
The title, abstract, and conclusion must describe the same paper. The abstract
should state the problem, bounded approach, strongest supported evidence, and
qualified takeaway without importing unsupported generality.

## Introduction and related work

Explain why the problem matters, what concrete limitation remains, and how ARIS
responds. Compare related systems only on dimensions supported by cited sources.
Do not infer absence, superiority, or causal mechanisms from title relevance or
metadata alone.

## Method

Present components in the order needed to understand the system. Distinguish
the documented default configuration from optional settings, and distinguish
the architecture from claims about its effectiveness. Keep names, notation,
figures, and tables aligned with the registered interfaces.

## Evidence and results

Lead with the question, state the observed result and conditions, then provide a
bounded interpretation. Separate reported observations from proposed
mechanisms. Negative or inconclusive evidence remains visible when it limits a
central claim.

## Limitations and conclusion

Limitations must state what the evidence cannot establish, especially causal or
general performance conclusions. The conclusion may synthesize established
findings but must not introduce new evidence or silently strengthen the paper's
scope.

## Local review

After editing, check the touched claim, number, citation, terminology, figure or
table reference, and variant behavior. A local section pass is not permission to
invoke a manuscript-wide reviewer or to approve publication.
